# PIXEL CONGREGATION · 像素集会

*An aesthetic movement for the FztbuCS Computer Science Association — manifest, 2026*

---

Every member is a pixel. Alone, each is a block of color, meaningless at arm's length; arranged with intent, they become a face, a letter, a world. The pixel is the smallest unit of digital truth — indivisible, honest, and useless in isolation. This movement treats the community itself as a congregation of pixels: 298 individual cells, each carrying exactly one color, assembling by shared rhythm into an image that none of them can see alone. The design therefore never *illustrates* the community; it *is* the community, rendered at the lowest possible resolution where the whole still resolves.

The canvas is a retina held up to its own screen. We work in deliberate low resolution — cells coarse enough to count, grid lines visible enough to honor — because truth lives at the boundary where order and noise are indistinguishable. Zoom in, and the image dissolves into data; zoom out, and the data resolves into image. This zoom is the movement's central ritual: every composition is built to be legible at a distance and dissolvable up close, so that the viewer must physically move to understand. Proximity and distance are not viewing options; they are the argument.

Color is rationed like scripture. Each work draws from a palette of two or three hues, held at near-full saturation against near-black, because a congregation of thousands only coheres when every cell speaks the same language. The palette whispers the association's identity: ink-blue for the night of computation, amber for the first light of a working build, the pink of a dawn gradient for the horizon of becoming. No gradients are wasted on surfaces — color exists only as discrete cells, so that what appears to be a smooth dawn is, up close, a census of individual decisions.

Composition obeys the mathematics of belonging. Primary forms — the Möbius ring, the sunrise, the recursive pixel — are plotted as coordinate truth, then snapped to a strict cell grid, so that every glowing element occupies a whole number of cells and nothing floats in the space between. Noise is permitted, but only as isolated cells breaking rank by design: one amber cell in a field of ink, one lit pixel among the dormant. These are the members who raised their hands. The grid is not a cage; it is the constitution they all signed.

Typography is scarce, and it knows its place. Words appear as coordinates, serial numbers, single declarative phrases — the language of instrument panels and log files, never paragraphs. Latin pixel typesetting (Silkscreen, Pixelify) carries the mechanical voice; a single small Songti seal in Chinese anchors the work in its place and year. Text never explains the image, because the image is already a statement; text merely stamps the canvas with provenance, like a signature on a photograph of a crowd.

The final work must bear the marks of obsession. Every pixel block is aligned to the grid to the pixel, every line is a whole-cell multiple, every gap is the same gap. The piece should look less like it was generated and more like it was *tiled* — by hand, cell by cell, by someone who counted every coordinate and will notice if one is off. It is the product of deep expertise and painstaking attention, a master-level execution where the craft is not in what is drawn, but in what is *refused*: the extra color, the extra word, the extra pixel that would have broken the congregation's silence.

*Movement born 2026.08.17, Fuzhou. To be expressed in three canvases: the ring, the dawn, the recursion.*
